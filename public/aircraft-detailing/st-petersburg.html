What flies from Albert Whitted

The longest runway at SPG is 3,676 feet, so the field sees mostly turboprops, light jets and helicopters rather than large cabins. On our price list that's the first two aircraft sizes and both helicopter sizes.

How access works

The City of St. Petersburg adopted new minimum standards for the airport on May 14, 2026. Cleaning, washing and waxing are named in them as aircraft services, and anyone providing them commercially needs an agreement with the City and a Commercial Operator Permit.

The standards encourage outside service providers to work as sublessees of the FBO, and there is also a temporary permit, usually 30 days or less, that the aircraft owner or operator starts. We tell you which of these applies before we book.

Washing rules

Albert Whitted's rules say plainly that aircraft may be dry washed without the City's approval. Washing that creates runoff outside an approved wash rack needs written approval first, and residual fluids go to an oil-water separator or off the airport. Our standard exterior is a dry wash, so it fits the rule as written.

Same prices at every airport. A midsize jet, for example: turn clean $1,250, exterior dry wash $3,150, full interior $3,850. See every size on the rate card.

Questions we get

Can you dry wash an aircraft at Albert Whitted?

Yes. The airport's rules allow dry washing without the City's approval. Wet washing outside an approved wash rack needs written approval first.

Do outside detailers need a permit at Albert Whitted?

Yes. Under the City's May 2026 minimum standards, commercial service providers need an agreement and a Commercial Operator Permit, or work as a sublessee of the FBO. A temporary permit started by the aircraft owner also exists for short-term work.

What aircraft can you detail at SPG?

Anything that operates there, which is mostly turboprops, light jets and helicopters because the longest runway is 3,676 feet. The rate card has the price for each size.
